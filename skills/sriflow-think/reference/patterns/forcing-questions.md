# Forcing-Question Patterns

A startup diagnostic in the YC mold. sriflow-think takes up the forcing-question
technique and the challenge posture, with external runtime dependencies
stripped out.

## The stance

The value here is the hard questions. Comfortable answers mean the founder has
not dug deep enough. In sriflow-think that maps straight onto stakeholder
discovery: comfortable stakeholder answers ("users want X", "the business
needs Y") are group labels, and group labels are RED in the register.

## The six forcing questions

Ask **one at a time**, pressing until the answer is specific and grounded in
evidence. Route by stage — you do not always need all six:

| Stage | Questions |
|-------|-----------|
| Pre-product | Q1, Q2, Q3 |
| Has users | Q2, Q4, Q5 |
| Has paying customers | Q4, Q5, Q6 |
| Pure engineering/infra | Q2, Q4 only |

**Q1 — Demand reality.** *What is the strongest evidence that someone genuinely
wants this — not that they are "interested", but that they would be upset if it
disappeared tomorrow?* Push for behavior: paying, growing usage, building a
workflow around it. **Red flag:** "people say it's interesting", waitlist signups,
"VCs are excited."

**Q2 — Status quo.** *What are users doing right now, even poorly, and what
does that workaround cost them?* Push for a workflow, hours, dollars. **Red
flag:** "nothing exists" — usually a sign the problem is not painful enough.
This maps to the market-research status-quo finding (04b).

**Q3 — Desperate specificity.** *Name the actual human. What is their title?
What gets them promoted? What gets them fired?* **Red flag:** category answers —
"healthcare enterprises", "SMBs", "marketing teams". You cannot email a category.
This is the forcing exemplar behind Step 2's "name the individual" rule.

**Q4 — Narrowest wedge.** *What is the smallest version someone would pay real
money for this week?* Push for one feature, one workflow, something shippable
in days. **Red flag:** "we need the full platform first."

**Q5 — Observation & surprise.** *Have you watched someone use this without
helping? What surprised you?* Push for a specific contradiction. **Red flag:**
"we sent a survey" — surveys lie and demos are theater. Gold: users doing
something the product was never designed for.

**Q6 — Future-fit.** *If the world looks different in three years, does this
become more essential or less?* Push for a specific claim about how the
user's world changes. **Red flag:** "AI keeps getting better so we do too" —
that is a rising tide any competitor can ride.

## Inversion ("what would make this fail?")

Used in uncertainty mapping (07b): for every high-stakes uncertainty, name
the failure mode. Catastrophic failures (data loss, regulatory, irreversible
commitment) force the uncertainty up a tier.

## 10-star thinking ("what's the 10x version?")

Used in Step 1 context (Q3): when the project phase is greenfield, ask what
the 10x version of the idea is before locking scope. This catches
self-limiting framing early and feeds the uncertainty register.

## Escape hatches (adopted)

- If the user resists the questions, ask the two most critical remaining ones,
  then move on. A second resistance is honored — stop asking.
- A full skip is allowed only when a fully formed plan exists (existing users,
  revenue, named customers).
- If earlier answers already cover a later question, skip it.
